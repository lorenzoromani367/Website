When a thing breaks, withers, or tears away, it turns into something different.

Most of the time, a discarded scrap is simply that. But taken aside and observed closely, certain fragments begin to show another side of themselves, carrying their own volume, friction, and presence.

They have no agency in where they end up; a stray breeze is enough to displace them.
For me, photographing them is a way of taking their side. I recognize that position, standing outside of any whole. Looking at them as they appear, without needing to label or justify them, brings an unexpected relief.
