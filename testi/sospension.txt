Most images are made to communicate: they deliver a message, prove a point, or stand for a specific idea. Instinctively, we look at them wanting to decide where we stand toward whatever we see. 

This ongoing project forms the foundation of an upcoming book: conceived not as a collection of photographs, but as an atlas of conditions that deliberately break away from that habit. It doesn’t document occurrences and offers no narrative cues to resolve. Instead, it maps situations where nothing appears to happen and nothing seems destined to begin, letting action and obvious meaning recede entirely. 

It is not a void, but a dense stillness.

Things existing with their own weight, utterly indifferent to any storyline.

The purpose of the project is to suspend narrative entirely, allowing the story to belong to the observer and severing the image even from the lived experience of those who were there. 

Looking ceases to be an act of deciphering and understanding; it becomes an exercise of pure imagination, an opening to invent something entirely personal. 

The physical matter before the camera is merely an anchor. The real subject only takes shape in the mind of the viewer.
