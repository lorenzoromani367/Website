Every gesture is typically understood and therefore extinguished, by its destination, subsumed under its practical function.
 
This project intervenes in that closure by isolating intentional movements and excising the moment that reveals their conclusion. This is not an interruption, which merely leaves behind the readable absence of an unfulfilled act, but a liberation: stripping movement of its finality to return it to pure form, a means without end where the body exhibits its presence in the world prior to any destination.

The research stems from late 19th-century photographic studies by Gilles de la Tourette, whose neurological patients exhibited gestures devoid of a recognizable end, opening the image to unbounded readings, dance, mourning, prayer, or waiting. By tracing the inverse path, photographically stripping a purposeful gesture of its resolution, these images restore movement to a state prior to classification. Incompleteness is not a flaw; it is the image's most expressive condition.

(Selection from an ongoing body of work)
