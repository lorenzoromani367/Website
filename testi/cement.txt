One of the quiet crises of our time is a perceptual estrangement from reality: a progressive loss of the ability to inhabit our surroundings and ourselves. Where we once expanded outward, we now contract, driven by an insatiable hunger for the unattainable, while genuine presence gives way to performance and external validation. Identity becomes displaced, defined by how we are seen rather than what we live, while time ceases to flow, thickening around an isolated body.

These works bridge figurative painting and concrete to give physical form to this internal fracture. The figures emerge directly from this state of disconnection: fragmented anatomies and unstable postures attempting to reconstruct an inhabitable corporeality.

Concrete embodies this tension. Associated with mass and permanence, it proves paradoxically fragile, cracking, eroding, and resisting control just like perception itself. Each piece functions as the residue of an enduring suspension: a threshold where the body persists and space endures, yet the living bond between them remains interrupted.
