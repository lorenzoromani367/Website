Framed at a precise moment, and in a certain light, a building can slip away from its familiar definition.

I like to wait for that routine to clear, looking for the brief window when the structure stands suspended, caught on the threshold between what took place and what is about to happen again.

I photograph these structures as solitary presences in a landscape of light and stillness.

While the captions anchor each building to its factual reality, what interests me is how little the physical surface reveals of it. Carrying no visible trace of their intended function or the events they contained, they stand indifferent to their purpose, leaving us with their bare presence, shaped by light, time, and something else I’ll never entirely pin down.
