Habit blinds us to the shape of a house, reducing it to the life that happens inside.
Without that domestic noise, the structure reclaims its own weight.

I like to wait for that routine to clear, looking for the brief window when the building stands suspended, caught on the threshold between what took place and what is about to happen again.

I photograph these structures as solitary presences in a landscape of light and stillness.

While the captions anchor each building to its specific reality, what interests me is how certain spaces seem to become something else entirely from what they are there for.

Carrying no visible trace of their intended function or the events they contained, they stand indifferent to their original purpose, leaving us with their bare presence, shaded by light, time, and something else I’ll never entirely pin down.
