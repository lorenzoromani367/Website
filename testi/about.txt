I work mainly with film because they fail in a very good way.
The grain, the loss of detail, the colors that slide slightly off,
these are not flaws but the conditions under which a photograph
starts to resemble how things actually look when you pay attention to them.
Digital imaging has become too precise, too complete
and it has stopped corresponding to reality.

My background is in painting, and the two practices focus on the same aspects:
suspensions, fragmentations, incomplete gestures,
surfaces or forms that carry a meaning that is not expected.
These themes move between paint and film depending on what the subject requires.
I take pictures on film when the grain and the color shift will enrich the work,
whereas I use digital when the subject needs precision,
and the control over light, color and detail that comes with it.

I now work from Athens.

What I find here, and nowhere else quite like this,
is that the decadence, hidden or visible,
seems to give me a good deal of hope for the future.
This tension runs through most of what I make.
